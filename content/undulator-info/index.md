{{< fold "What to Bring" >}}

The Aorangi Undulator is a backcountry event, and conditions can change quickly. Come prepared.

- **Essential gear:** the full minimum list is on our [race regulations page](/racing/#essential-gear). Gear may be spot-checked.
- **Your best trail shoes.** Expect wet, slippery, technical river travel, mud, scree and precipitous descents, with very limited course marking. This is a route, not a track.

## Course

{{< fold "Course Information" "open" >}}

Point-to-point: **Mangatoetoe → Putangirua Pinnacles**, **~27 km / 2,000 m ascent**.

- **Route map:** [PDF](AorangiUndulatorMap.pdf)
- **GPX file:** [download](/undulator/undulator.gpx). Load it onto your phone's mapping app, fully charged, *before* race day.
- **Course marking is limited.** Marshals are at the huts and the Pinnacles turn-off only.

{{< highlight-box >}}
**⚠ Weather contingency:** if conditions are unfavourable, the race may be cancelled or switched to the B-Course. We'll know in advance, and will confirm the day before with any updated route.

## Course Description

{{< fold "#1 — Ngā Rā o Kupe to Kawakawa Hut" >}}

From Ngā Rā o Kupe, pick your way up the Mangatoetoe on any of the many braided lines to Mangatoetoe Hut, then continue up the correct stream.

Within 4–5 km the Mangatoetoe branches multiple times. Take care to follow the large orange DOC triangles.

The valley closes in and turns gorge-like. Travel up the river, dipping in and out of it, following the orange markers where you can. Where the trail exits the river, it's well bedded and marked.

A steep climb of roughly 130 m puts you over the saddle. The first undulation is brief, but the descent into the next catchment, Otakaha Stream, is precipitous. Take care. It ends at a waterfall and pool.

Follow the Otakaha downstream, boulder-hopping for 4 km, then start scanning the true right. You may see shortcut routes, and a large DOC triangle marks where you leave the riverbed. Miss it and you'll carry on down the river, past Kawakawa Hut. Go through a copse of trees on a well-cleared track, and continue along the flats to Kawakawa Hut.

{{< endfold >}}

{{< fold "#2 — Kawakawa Hut to Pararaki Hut" >}}

Kawakawa Hut sits on the true right of the river, where a 4WD track crosses. **You must get checked by the marshal here.** Note the 3-hour cut-off.

Head upstream, crossing the creek regularly, for 10–15 minutes. Turn left at the wooden "Pararaki Hut" sign.

The next undulation follows a fine knife edge between tributaries: roughly 310 m of climbing, steeply up a narrow ridge (be careful, don't fall off), then a long cambered sidle to the saddle. It's awkward, off-camber work through native ongaonga and rocky shingle.

Over the saddle, descend to the Pararaki. Turn right (upstream) and follow it to Pararaki Hut.

{{< endfold >}}

{{< fold "#3 — Pararaki Hut to Washpool Hut" >}}

Pararaki Hut is 100 m upstream, on the left (true right). **You must get checked by the marshal here.**

A big hill: roughly 550 m of climbing. There's windfall at the base of the track, not far from the hut. Follow the orange tape and markers through it. From there it's a solid climb on an obvious track, opening out through beech forest with plenty of tree fall.

Then a very steep descent, scary in full send, ending in a final 30-odd metres of loose scree where it's very easy to gather momentum, wanted or otherwise.

Turn left at Washpool Stream and follow the blazes downstream for 100 m. Look for the sign to the hut on the true right bank, then head up the steps to Washpool Hut.

{{< endfold >}}

{{< fold "#4 — Washpool Hut to Finish" >}}

**You must get checked by the marshals at Washpool Hut.**

Much like the last: follow the well-signed Kopi track up, up and up. Approximately 550 m of climbing, steeply to the summit, then an obvious left onto the NW ridge. If you can afford the time, a brief dogleg at the top gives good views of the unnumbered undulations to come.

The ridge undulates for 3 km through bush: false peaks, all of them brief. When you reach the old fence and 4x4 track, the climbing is pretty much over.

Descend through Gorse Alley. Like taking off a plaster, this is best done quickly. Showing weakness will be booed.

Take the signed (and marshalled) left turn to the Pinnacles. **You must check in with the marshal.** Turn left onto the lookout track, NOT the Circular Walk, and descend the obvious stepped route to the river valley. The course is bookended with geology: the Putangirua Pinnacles at one end, Ngā Rā o Kupe at the other.

Turn right at the river valley and follow the river downstream, towards the aroma of sizzling sausages and the rest awaiting you.

Look out for **BLUE WARATAHS WITH ORANGE TRIANGLES.** There are two, and they'll put you on the track to the finish. Miss them and it's not a major. You'll just be on rougher terrain, and it'll take longer.

You're done. Go party. You are not weak.
